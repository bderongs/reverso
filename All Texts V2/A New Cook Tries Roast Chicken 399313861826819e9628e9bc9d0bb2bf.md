## Text

On Sunday, Maya decided to <em>roast</em> a whole chicken for her parents, although she had never done it alone before. She read the <em>recipe</em> twice, then used a <em>garlic press</em> for the cloves and <em>mixed</em> them with butter, salt, and pepper to <em>season</em> the bird.

Before putting it in the <em>oven</em>, she <em>trimmed</em> the extra fat near the opening and <em>drizzled</em> olive oil over the skin. After an hour, the kitchen smelled wonderful, but Maya worried the meat might still be <em>undercooked</em>, so she checked it with a <em>thermometer</em>.

The number was perfect, and the chicken looked golden instead of pale or <em>overcooked</em>. While it rested, she made <em>gravy</em> from the pan juices, <em>stirring</em> carefully until it became smooth.

At the table, her father showed her how to <em>carve</em> the breast and legs without tearing the meat. The first bite was <em>tender</em> and fully <em>cooked</em>, and Maya smiled when everyone asked for seconds.
