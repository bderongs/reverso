## Text

Last year, my closest friend, Maya, shared a private story about me at a party. I felt <em>betrayed</em> because I had trusted her, and her words <em>hurt</em> me more than she realized. When people laughed, I was <em>embarrassed</em>, then deeply <em>humiliated</em>.

For weeks, she avoided me, which made the <em>shame</em> even worse. I wondered if she felt <em>guilty</em>, or if my pain simply did not matter to her. I carried a heavy sense of <em>guilt</em> too, because part of me regretted telling her the secret in the first place.

One afternoon, Maya asked to meet at a quiet café. She looked <em>ashamed</em> and <em>regretful</em> as she explained that she had spoken without thinking. Her voice shook with <em>remorse</em>, and she said her biggest <em>regret</em> was damaging our friendship.

I did not forgive her immediately, but I was <em>relieved</em> to hear an honest apology. Over time, I became <em>thankful</em> that we had talked instead of staying silent. Trust returned slowly, but the conversation taught us both to treat people’s feelings with more care.
