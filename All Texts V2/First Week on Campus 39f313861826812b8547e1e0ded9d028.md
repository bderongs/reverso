## Text

On Monday morning, I arrived on <em>campus</em> with two suitcases and a nervous smile. During <em>orientation</em>, a senior student showed us the main <em>facility</em>, the <em>library</em>, and the <em>auditorium</em> where the <em>dean</em> welcomed every new <em>undergraduate</em>.

My <em>roommate</em>, Daniel, was already unpacking when I reached our room, and he had taped a schedule above his desk. The next day, we walked to our first <em>classroom</em> together and met a <em>classmate</em> from Spain who was also <em>studying</em> biology.

By Wednesday, <em>college</em> life felt less confusing. A helpful <em>peer</em> explained how to book study rooms in the <em>library</em>, and several <em>faculty</em> members invited students to visit during office hours.

At the end of the week, I sat in the cafeteria and realized I had learned more than directions and <em>course</em> names. I had begun to understand the rhythm of academic life: asking questions, managing time, and finding support in unexpected places.
