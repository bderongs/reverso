## Text

As the <em>semester</em> reaches its halfway point, many students begin organizing their schedules around every <em>midterm</em>, <em>exam</em>, and <em>quiz</em> on the calendar. Academic advisers recommend that students <em>study</em> in short, regular sessions rather than <em>cram</em> the night before a test.

One useful strategy is to <em>take notes</em> during <em>lectures</em> and review them within 24 hours, while the material is still fresh. Students can also <em>jot down</em> key definitions, formulas, and questions in a separate notebook to help them <em>memorize</em> important concepts without feeling overwhelmed.

For many learners, a <em>study group</em> provides structure and motivation, especially when members explain difficult ideas to one another. Regular <em>practice</em> with sample questions can reveal weak areas early, giving students time to improve before <em>grades</em> are final.

Although every student hopes to <em>pass</em>, one disappointing score does not have to define the <em>semester</em>. If students <em>fail</em> an assessment, they should speak with the instructor, learn from their mistakes, and ask whether they can <em>retake</em> it or complete extra work.
