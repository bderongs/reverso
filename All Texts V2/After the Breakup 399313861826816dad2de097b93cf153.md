## Text

When Maya and Leo <em>split up</em> after four years together, their <em>friends</em> expected drama, but the <em>breakup</em> was <em>quiet</em>. They had not stopped <em>caring</em> overnight; they had simply <em>grown apart</em>, <em>arguing</em> about small things and avoiding the bigger truth.

For weeks, Maya felt a heavy <em>grief</em> that surprised her. She would stare at his old messages, <em>fighting</em> the <em>longing</em> to write, “I <em>miss you</em>,” even though she knew it might <em>break her heart</em> all over again.

Leo tried dating someone new <em>on the rebound</em>, but every conversation felt unfair to the other person. He realized he had not <em>fallen out of love</em> completely; he only wanted the pain to move faster than it could.

They met once in a café to exchange books and finally spoke honestly. There was no dramatic <em>reconciliation</em>, but the conversation gave them both <em>closure</em>, and Maya began to believe she could <em>get over</em> him without <em>pretending</em> the <em>relationship</em> had meant nothing.
