## Text

Clothes shopping becomes easier when you understand the <em>style</em> <em>labels</em> used by stores and fashion writers. A <em>casual</em> <em>outfit</em> usually means relaxed pieces such as <em>jeans</em>, trainers, or a <em>soft</em> <em>cotton</em> <em>shirt</em>, while <em>dressy</em> clothing is meant for dinners, interviews, or special events.

Some <em>labels</em> describe a mood. <em>Trendy</em> <em>items</em> follow what is popular right now, but they may feel <em>old-fashioned</em> next season. <em>Retro</em> refers to clothing inspired by past decades, such as 1970s <em>jackets</em> or 1990s <em>denim</em>.

Other words suggest attitude. An <em>edgy</em> look might include <em>leather</em>, <em>bold</em> cuts, or unexpected details, while a <em>preppy</em> <em>style</em> often features polo <em>shirts</em>, loafers, and neat <em>sweaters</em>. <em>Flashy</em> clothes attract attention with bright colours, shine, or large logos.

If you prefer simplicity, <em>minimalist</em> <em>outfits</em> use clean lines and few accessories. <em>Understated</em> clothing is quiet and elegant rather than loud, and <em>sophisticated</em> pieces look <em>polished</em> and mature. A <em>timeless</em> <em>coat</em> or black <em>dress</em> stays useful for years because it never really goes out of <em>style</em>.
