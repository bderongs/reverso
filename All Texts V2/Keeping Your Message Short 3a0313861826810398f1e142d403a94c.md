## Text

In many workplaces, the best presentations are not the longest ones. A <em>succinct</em> speaker respects the audience’s time, states the <em>headline</em> early, and makes every <em>slide</em> support one clear <em>key point</em>.

Before you present, ask yourself what the <em>gist</em> of your message is. <em>In a nutshell</em>, your audience should be able to repeat your main idea after one minute; <em>in brief</em>, they need direction, not decoration.

A <em>pithy</em> opening can help people focus immediately. <em>For example</em>, <em>instead</em> of giving a long background story, <em>cut to the chase</em> and say, “Sales fell 12%, and here is how we can recover.”

During the presentation, use signposting phrases such as <em>in short</em>, “This change saves time,” or <em>long story short</em>, “The old system is slowing us down.” These phrases help you <em>get to the point</em> without sounding rude.

The <em>bottom line</em> is simple: clear language builds confidence. When your message is brief, organized, and memorable, people are more likely to listen, understand, and act.
