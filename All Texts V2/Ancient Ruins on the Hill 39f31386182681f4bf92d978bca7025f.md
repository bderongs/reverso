## Text

Travelers arriving in the valley often notice the <em>massive</em> stone walls long before they reach the village. The old <em>fortress</em> rises above the road, its <em>dramatic</em> outline marking one of the region’s most visited <em>historic sites</em>.

At the top, a narrow path leads through the remains of a <em>citadel</em>, where signs explain the original <em>layout</em> of streets, gates, and defensive <em>towers</em>. A small <em>temple</em> <em>ruin</em> stands near the center, with carved <em>columns</em> that still show traces of skilled craftsmanship.

Beyond the main gate, visitors can follow a shaded trail to a former <em>monastery</em> built into the hillside. From this quiet spot, the <em>surroundings</em> open into wide fields, olive groves, and distant <em>mountains</em>.

The best <em>viewpoint</em> is reached just before sunset, when golden light falls across the stones and the valley below. Local guides recommend bringing water, comfortable shoes, and enough time to explore slowly.
