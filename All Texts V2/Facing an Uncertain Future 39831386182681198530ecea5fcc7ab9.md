## Text

As graduation approaches, many young adults are living with a strange mix of <em>hope</em> and anxiety. The job market looks promising in some fields, yet <em>hopeless</em> in others, leaving students <em>conflicted</em> about which path to follow.

At a recent career forum, several graduates said they felt <em>overwhelmed</em> by advice from parents, teachers, and social media. One student admitted feeling <em>vulnerable</em> when comparing herself with classmates, while another described moments of <em>despair</em> after receiving repeated rejections.

Still, the atmosphere was not entirely negative. Many participants said they remained <em>hopeful</em>, <em>motivated</em> by small successes, and <em>inspired</em> by stories of people who changed direction and eventually built meaningful careers.

Career counselor Maya Lewis encouraged students to be <em>skeptical</em> of perfect success stories online but <em>eager</em> to learn from real experience. She said <em>anticipation</em> can be useful when it pushes people to prepare, but dangerous when it becomes <em>fear</em>.

For those feeling <em>despondent</em>, her advice was simple: take one practical step each day. “Uncertainty is uncomfortable,” she said, “but it can also be the beginning of growth.”
