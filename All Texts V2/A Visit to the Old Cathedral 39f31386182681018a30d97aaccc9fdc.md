## Text

Last Saturday, I joined a small walking tour through the <em>historic</em> center of the city. Our guide led us into a quiet <em>cathedral</em> near the main square, where the cool air and solemn <em>atmosphere</em> made everyone lower their voices.

Inside, the <em>Gothic</em> <em>arches</em> rose above us, supported by each <em>massive</em> <em>column</em> along the nave. Sunlight passed through the <em>stained glass</em>, covering the stone floor with blue, red, and gold patterns.

The guide explained that the building had once been part of a larger <em>basilica</em>, although only one <em>chapel</em> from the original structure remains. In that <em>chapel</em>, I noticed an <em>ornate</em> wooden <em>carving</em> of saints and angels, worn smooth by centuries of touch.

Before leaving, we stepped outside to admire the tall <em>spire</em> and the green copper <em>dome</em> above the roof. From the steps, the whole place looked <em>majestic</em>, and I felt as if I had briefly traveled into another century.
