## Text

In strong <em>relationships</em>, a <em>boundary</em> is not a wall but a clear line that protects each person’s emotional space. <em>Mutual</em> <em>respect</em> and <em>consent</em> help partners feel safe enough to express needs without fear of being judged.

Couples who <em>communicate</em> honestly can prevent small <em>misunderstandings</em> from becoming lasting resentment. It is equally important to <em>listen</em>, <em>empathize</em>, and offer <em>support</em> when a partner feels stressed, insecure, or overwhelmed.

<em>Vulnerability</em> often grows slowly, especially for someone who finds it difficult to <em>open up</em>. A caring partner can <em>reassure</em> them with patience, while still being honest about their own limits.

Problems may appear when one person becomes overly <em>needy</em>, <em>controlling</em>, or <em>co-dependent</em>. <em>Love</em> feels healthier when both people can stay <em>connected</em> while also keeping their <em>independence</em>, <em>friendships</em>, and personal goals.
