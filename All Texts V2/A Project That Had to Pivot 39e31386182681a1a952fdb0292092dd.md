## Text

On Monday morning, our software team learned that a new privacy rule would <em>impede</em> the launch of a client portal we had spent six months building. <em>Calling off</em> the project was <em>off the table</em>, but the sponsor agreed to put the public release on hold.

At first, it felt like a <em>step back</em>, and some engineers feared we were going <em>back to square one</em>. Our project manager asked us to <em>deal with</em> the <em>risk</em> calmly, list every blocked feature, and identify a practical <em>remedy</em> for each one.

By Wednesday, we found a <em>workaround</em>: users could verify their identity through an existing secure service while we redesigned the original login flow. This allowed us to <em>pivot</em> without wasting the research, designs, and vendor contracts already completed.

The revised plan was <em>on the table</em> by Friday, with a smaller pilot scheduled for two departments. Although the <em>delay</em> was frustrating, the crisis helped us <em>lay the groundwork</em> for a safer, more reliable product.
