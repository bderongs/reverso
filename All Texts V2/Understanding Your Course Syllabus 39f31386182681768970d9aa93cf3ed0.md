## Text

At the start of every <em>semester</em>, students receive a <em>syllabus</em> that explains how a <em>course</em> is organized. It usually lists the instructor’s contact details, required <em>textbook</em>, weekly <em>lecture</em> topics, and important university policies.

A careful reading can prevent confusion later. The document often describes each <em>assignment</em>, the expected <em>homework</em>, and the percentage of the final <em>grade</em> connected to <em>exams</em>, projects, and class participation.

Students should also check rules about <em>attendance</em>, late <em>submission</em>, and academic honesty. In many classes, regular participation and timely <em>completion</em> of <em>coursework</em> can make a noticeable difference, especially when the material becomes more challenging.

Keeping the <em>syllabus</em> nearby is a practical habit. Before emailing a <em>professor</em> with a question, review it first; many answers about deadlines, readings, and grading procedures are already clearly stated there.
