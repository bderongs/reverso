## Text

In many offices, a difficult manager can shape the mood of an entire team. Staff may arrive motivated, but a <em>controlling</em> leader who checks every detail and questions every decision can quickly make people feel powerless.

One employee described her supervisor as <em>strict</em> and <em>stern</em> in meetings, but also <em>bossy</em> in daily conversations. He was <em>demanding</em> about deadlines, <em>pushy</em> about overtime, and often <em>judgmental</em> when someone suggested a different approach.

Colleagues said his most damaging habit was his <em>condescending</em> tone. Even experienced workers felt small when he explained simple tasks as if no one else was competent.

Over time, his <em>arrogant</em> and <em>conceited</em> behavior became <em>intimidating</em>, especially for younger staff who were afraid to make mistakes. Productivity did not improve; instead, people stopped sharing ideas, avoided responsibility, and quietly looked for other jobs.

Workplace experts say strong leadership requires clear expectations, not fear. A manager can be firm without humiliating others, and <em>confident</em> without acting superior.
