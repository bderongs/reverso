## Text

Mina was the <em>shy</em> girl in our evening English class, always sitting near the window with a <em>neat</em> notebook and a <em>reserved</em> smile. She was <em>soft-spoken</em> during pair work, and sometimes so <em>taciturn</em> that new students assumed she had little to say.

In fact, Mina was deeply <em>sensitive</em> and <em>thoughtful</em>, noticing when classmates felt nervous before presentations. She was also <em>clever</em> and <em>astute</em>; while others memorized speeches, she studied the audience and predicted which examples would make people listen.

When our teacher announced a debate contest, nobody expected Mina to enter. Yet she was <em>determined</em> to prove that quiet people could be powerful speakers, and her <em>tenacious</em> practice turned nervous whispers into clear arguments.

On contest day, Mina walked to the front looking pale but <em>brave</em>. Her <em>plucky</em> opening line made the room laugh, and by the end, even the loudest students were taking notes from her calm, precise style.
