## Text

In many schools, <em>technology</em> is no longer a special addition but a regular part of classroom life. Tablets, online libraries, and learning platforms can <em>transform</em> the way students access information and complete assignments.

One important <em>feature</em> of digital tools is their ability to give quick feedback, which may <em>enhance</em> motivation and help learners correct mistakes sooner. Another useful <em>function</em> is allowing students to <em>interact</em> with simulations, videos, and quizzes that explain <em>complex</em> ideas in clearer ways.

The <em>potential</em> <em>benefits</em> are especially <em>evident</em> in subjects such as science and history, where visual material can make abstract topics easier to understand. However, the <em>final</em> <em>outcome</em> depends on how teachers use these tools, not simply on the devices themselves.

Researchers note that <em>influential</em> teachers combine digital <em>resources</em> with discussion, reading, and critical thinking <em>tasks</em>. When used carefully, educational <em>technology</em> can support deeper learning, but it should not replace human guidance or meaningful communication.
