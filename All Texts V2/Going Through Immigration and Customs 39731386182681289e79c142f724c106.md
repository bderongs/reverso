## Text

For many international passengers, the first step after <em>arrival</em> is to follow the signs to <em>immigration</em>. An officer will check your <em>passport</em>, ask about your <em>destination</em>, and may confirm whether you have a valid <em>visa</em> for the country.

Travelers should answer questions clearly and honestly, especially if they are entering a <em>foreign</em> country for work, study, or tourism. Officials may also remind visitors not to <em>overstay</em> the time allowed, because doing so can lead to fines or future entry problems.

After the <em>border</em> check, passengers collect their <em>baggage</em> and continue to <em>customs</em>. This is where you may need to <em>declare</em> food, alcohol, gifts, large amounts of cash, or expensive <em>valuables</em> bought <em>abroad</em>.

Rules vary from country to country, so it is wise to read the form carefully before leaving the airport. Being prepared helps the process move quickly and makes the start of your <em>trip</em> much less stressful.
