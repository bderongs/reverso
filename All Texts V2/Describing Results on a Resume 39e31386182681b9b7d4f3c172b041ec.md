## Text

Hiring managers look for evidence, not just responsibilities. Instead of saying you helped with sales, explain how you <em>managed</em> a client portfolio, <em>generated</em> new leads, and <em>achieved</em> measurable growth.

Strong <em>resume</em> statements often begin with action verbs. You might write that you <em>developed</em> a training program, <em>implemented</em> a new scheduling system, or <em>streamlined</em> a reporting process to save time and reduce errors.

Numbers make your experience more persuasive. If you helped <em>launch</em> a product, mention the <em>milestone</em> you reached, such as the first 1,000 users, and describe how you tracked progress to <em>optimize</em> future campaigns.

Your goal is to show a clear <em>track record</em> of results. For example, you can say you <em>expanded</em> the customer base, <em>maximized</em> team productivity, or <em>accomplished</em> a difficult project ahead of schedule.

Before an <em>interview</em>, review each bullet point and prepare a short story behind it. Employers want to understand what you did, why it mattered, and how your actions created <em>value</em>.
