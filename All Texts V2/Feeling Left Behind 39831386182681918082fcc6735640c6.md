## Text

When Maya’s closest friend, Lena, got promoted, everyone in the office gathered around her desk with flowers and cake. Maya smiled and clapped, but inside she felt <em>jealous</em> and strangely <em>envious</em>, as if Lena’s success had exposed her own lack of progress.

For the rest of the week, Maya became quiet and <em>distant</em>. She hated the <em>envy</em> rising in her whenever Lena spoke about new projects, and she felt <em>insecure</em> and <em>self-conscious</em> during meetings, convinced that others saw her as ordinary.

At first, Maya began to <em>resent</em> Lena, and her private thoughts turned <em>bitter</em> and <em>resentful</em>. When Lena once joked about being “too busy to breathe,” Maya thought she sounded <em>smug</em>, even though Lena probably meant no harm.

By Friday, Maya felt <em>discouraged</em> and <em>neglected</em>, especially when her manager praised Lena again. That evening, she finally admitted to herself that the problem was not Lena’s promotion, but her own <em>fear</em> of being left behind.
