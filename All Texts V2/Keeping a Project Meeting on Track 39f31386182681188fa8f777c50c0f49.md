## Text

Maya opened the Monday meeting with a shared <em>agenda</em> so everyone could <em>keep track</em> of <em>decisions</em>. When Tom began explaining last month’s client dinner, she smiled and said, “Let’s <em>cut to the chase</em> and <em>stick to</em> the launch plan.”

The team reviewed the project <em>scope</em> and <em>agreed</em> that the new payment feature was the main <em>priority</em>. Their <em>timeline</em> was tight, but the first <em>milestone</em>—testing the checkout page—was still possible by Friday.

Then Lena <em>raised an issue</em>: the design team had not received the final brand colors. That <em>roadblock</em> could delay the <em>deliverable</em>, so Maya assigned an <em>owner</em>, Ravi, to contact the client before noon.

Before closing, Maya asked everyone to name one <em>risk</em> that might affect the launch. By the end, the meeting felt focused, practical, and calm, and each person knew exactly what to do next.
