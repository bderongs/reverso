## Text

During the final month of a website redesign, Mia, the project manager, received a request from sales to add a customer <em>dashboard</em>. She reviewed the <em>scope</em> and realized the change would delay testing by two weeks, so she prepared a clear <em>rationale</em> for the <em>leadership</em> team.

In the meeting, Mia explained the <em>trade-off</em>: the <em>dashboard</em> could improve client demos, but it would threaten the launch date. Marketing said speed was the top <em>priority</em>, while sales argued for the new feature, so Mia helped both sides <em>negotiate</em> a <em>compromise</em>.

She asked the team to <em>push back</em> on any low-value extras and focus only on data that customers had requested. After a long discussion, they reached <em>consensus</em> and gained <em>buy-in</em> from the main stakeholders.

Mia documented the <em>decision</em>, requested formal <em>approval</em>, and reminded everyone that no developer would start work without <em>sign-off</em>. The project launched only three days late, and the <em>dashboard</em> became a useful addition rather than a chaotic surprise.
