## Text

At Monday’s project meeting, Maya noticed that Daniel looked unusually quiet. When the manager asked for updates, she <em>was all ears</em>, trying to <em>take in</em> every detail and <em>pick up on</em> the hesitation in his voice.

Daniel said the <em>deadline</em> was “ambitious,” but Maya could <em>read between the lines</em>. She had learned to keep her ears open in meetings, especially when people avoided saying exactly what they meant.

Later, a junior colleague told everyone that Daniel wanted to cancel the launch. Maya gently warned him not to <em>jump to conclusions</em> or take one sentence out of context, because <em>crossed wires</em> could damage trust.

After the meeting, Maya asked Daniel for five minutes to <em>clear up</em> the confusion. He explained that he only wanted extra testing, so she set the team straight before anyone could <em>miss the point</em>.

By the end of the day, everyone had <em>tuned in</em> to the real <em>concern</em>: quality, not delay. Maya’s careful listening helped the team move forward calmly.
