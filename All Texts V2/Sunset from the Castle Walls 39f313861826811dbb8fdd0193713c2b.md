## Text

Last autumn, I climbed the steep path to the old <em>castle</em> above the city just before evening. The <em>fortress</em> had survived wars and storms, and its tallest <em>tower</em> offered a <em>majestic</em> <em>view</em> of the streets below.

From the walls, the <em>landscape</em> opened wide, with red rooftops, a silver <em>river</em>, and dark hills along the <em>horizon</em>. I took one quick <em>glance</em> at the <em>map</em>, then let my <em>gaze</em> follow the light as it moved across the valley.

Near the main square, an <em>obelisk</em> stood beside a stone <em>monument</em> to the city’s founders. A small <em>memorial</em> with fresh flowers reminded visitors that the place was not only <em>beautiful</em>, but also full of difficult history.

As the sun dropped, the sky turned gold and violet, and the whole scene became <em>breathtaking</em>. I stayed until the lamps came on, feeling that <em>sightseeing</em> can be more than checking places off a list; sometimes it is a quiet conversation with the past.
