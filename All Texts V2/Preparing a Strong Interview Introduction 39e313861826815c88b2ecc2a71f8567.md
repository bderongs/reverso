## Text

A clear opening can shape the direction of an <em>interview</em> within the first minute. <em>Candidates</em> should briefly explain their <em>background</em>, mention one relevant <em>achievement</em>, and connect both to the role they want.

Instead of memorizing a speech, prepare a <em>flexible</em> summary of your <em>career path</em>. Focus on one <em>professional</em> experience that shows a key <em>strength</em>, such as problem-solving, teamwork, or leadership.

Your <em>objective</em> is to help the employer understand why you are <em>qualified</em> for the position. Describe your <em>skill set</em> with specific examples, not general claims, so your introduction sounds confident and believable.

It is also useful to include a sentence about your <em>ambition</em> and long-term <em>aspiration</em>. When your goals match the company’s needs, you appear more <em>motivated</em> and better prepared to contribute.
