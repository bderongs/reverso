## Text

Last month, I had to <em>travel</em> <em>overseas</em> for my cousin’s wedding in Canada. It was my first <em>trip</em> <em>abroad</em> alone, so I felt excited and slightly nervous as I <em>boarded</em> the <em>airplane</em> for the <em>long haul</em> across the Atlantic.

Because the ticket was cheaper, I booked a <em>red-eye</em> that left just before midnight. A few hours into the <em>journey</em>, the <em>captain</em> warned us about <em>turbulence</em>, and the <em>cabin</em> shook so much that several passengers stopped watching their films.

The <em>flight attendants</em> stayed calm, serving water and checking on a young child who was frightened by the sudden drops. I tried to sleep, but the <em>foreign</em> accents around me, the engine noise, and my own worries kept me awake.

After nearly eight hours, the clouds finally cleared, and we prepared for <em>touchdown</em> in Toronto. By the time I reached my hotel, <em>jet lag</em> hit me hard, but I still felt proud that I had managed the whole experience on my own.
