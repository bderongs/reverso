## Text

When a project is <em>behind schedule</em> and the <em>stakes are high</em>, a manager must <em>lead</em> with clarity rather than panic. The best leaders <em>take charge</em> early, define <em>priorities</em>, and make sure everyone understands what success looks like.

In many teams, one person may need to <em>take the lead</em> during a critical phase, but that does not mean they should <em>call the shots</em> alone. Strong project managers <em>empower</em> specialists to make informed <em>decisions</em>, which keeps the team <em>on the ball</em> and reduces <em>delays</em>.

To <em>motivate</em> people during stressful weeks, regular <em>feedback</em> and public <em>recognition</em> are essential. Even a short message praising a solved problem can boost confidence and help the team stay <em>ahead of the curve</em>.

<em>Progress</em> is not always dramatic, so managers should <em>focus on</em> actions that truly <em>move the needle</em>, such as removing blockers or improving communication. As the <em>deadline</em> approaches, the team can <em>finish up</em> with a clear handover, lessons learned, and a shared sense of achievement.
