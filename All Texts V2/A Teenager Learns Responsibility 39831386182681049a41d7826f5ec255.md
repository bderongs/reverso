## Text

At fifteen, Leo was known as the <em>naughty</em> kid in his apartment building. He enjoyed harmless <em>mischief</em>, like hiding his sister’s phone or leaving <em>goofy</em> notes on neighbors’ doors, but his <em>mischievous</em> jokes often went too far.

One Saturday, while being <em>silly</em> with a football in the hallway, Leo made a <em>foolish</em> kick and broke Mrs. Patel’s flowerpot. He tried to run, but his <em>clumsy</em> escape ended when he slipped on the stairs and landed beside the broken pieces.

His father didn’t shout; he simply said Leo was acting <em>immature</em> and needed to become more <em>responsible</em>. Feeling embarrassed, Leo knocked on Mrs. Patel’s door, offered an <em>earnest</em> apology, and promised to replace the pot with his own savings.

Over the next week, he helped her carry groceries and clean the hallway. The experience taught him <em>humility</em>, and he began to <em>mature</em> as he saw how his actions affected other people.
