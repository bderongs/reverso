## Text

Visitors to the old quarter can explore several sacred sites within a short walk of the central square. A <em>guided tour</em> usually begins at a <em>historic</em> <em>mosque</em>, where a wide <em>dome</em> and graceful <em>arches</em> show the city’s mixed <em>architecture</em>.

From there, the route continues to a small <em>synagogue</em> hidden behind a stone <em>courtyard</em>. Its <em>ornate</em> wooden doors and painted ceiling often surprise travelers who might otherwise pass by without noticing it.

A narrow street then leads to a quiet <em>chapel</em> beside a former <em>monastery</em>, now partly used as a cultural center. The garden there feels especially <em>peaceful</em>, with benches under cypress trees and <em>views</em> of the surrounding rooftops.

The final stop is a hilltop <em>temple</em> that looks over the <em>river</em> and the market below. Guides often remind visitors to dress respectfully, speak softly, and take time to observe how each place reflects centuries of faith, art, and community life.
