## Text

Last Tuesday, I went to the <em>clinic</em> because a <em>persistent</em> <em>ache</em> in my lower back had stopped me from sleeping. At first it was only a <em>mild</em>, <em>slight</em> <em>twinge</em> when I bent down, but by the weekend it had become a <em>constant</em> discomfort.

In the <em>examination</em> room, Dr. Patel asked me to describe the pain carefully. I told her it was sometimes <em>intermittent</em>, coming in waves, but during a <em>spasm</em> it turned <em>intense</em> and almost <em>severe</em>.

She asked whether it was <em>excruciating</em> or <em>unbearable</em>, and I said it had been close to that after I carried groceries upstairs. A warm shower helped <em>alleviate</em> it for a while, making it <em>tolerable</em>, but a <em>lingering</em> <em>soreness</em> always returned.

Dr. Patel checked my movement and explained that I had probably strained a muscle. She gave me exercises, <em>advised</em> rest, and told me to come back if the pain became sharper or did not improve within a week.
