## Text

Start your visit early at the main <em>parking lot</em>, where rangers post <em>maps</em>, weather updates, and advice on the best <em>hiking trail</em> for your ability. The area is <em>vast</em>, so choose one section rather than trying to see everything in a single day.

For easy <em>sightseeing</em>, follow the <em>river</em> path toward the <em>lake</em>, passing a shaded <em>picnic area</em> with tables and drinking water. A short side trail leads to a <em>waterfall</em>, where mist cools the rocks and photographers gather before noon.

More adventurous visitors can continue toward the <em>mountain</em> <em>viewpoint</em>, which offers <em>wide-open</em> <em>views</em> across the <em>canyon</em> and forest below. Nearby, a <em>guided tour</em> explores a limestone <em>cave</em>, a cool escape during hot afternoons.

Families staying overnight should book a site at the <em>campground</em> in advance, especially on summer weekends. Just beyond the boundary, a <em>wildlife refuge</em> protects birds, deer, and smaller animals, making the whole region ideal for nature lovers.
