## Text

Visitors entering the <em>palace</em> first step into a broad <em>courtyard</em>, where guides point out the mix of <em>baroque</em> decoration and <em>neoclassical</em> <em>architecture</em>. The stone façade is <em>impressive</em>, but the finest <em>detail</em> is often found above eye level, around carved windows and <em>statues</em>.

Inside, a sweeping <em>staircase</em> leads to the main <em>hall</em>, once used for official receptions and state dinners. Along the walls, a restored <em>mural</em> shows scenes from the kingdom’s history, with bright colors that still feel surprisingly fresh.

From a small <em>balcony</em>, tourists can look down over the ceremony rooms and imagine musicians playing during royal celebrations. The route then continues to a sunny <em>terrace</em>, offering a quiet <em>view</em> of the gardens and the city beyond.

<em>Museum</em> staff recommend taking time rather than rushing from room to room. For travelers interested in <em>sightseeing</em>, the building offers not only beauty but also a clear lesson in how power, taste, and history can shape a <em>landmark</em>.
