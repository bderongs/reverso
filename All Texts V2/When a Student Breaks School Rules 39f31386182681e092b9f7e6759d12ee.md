## Text

On Monday morning, our <em>teacher</em> noticed that Daniel’s <em>attendance</em> had become irregular and that he often arrived late to the <em>classroom</em>. She was <em>strict</em> about rules, but she also knew he had been under pressure since changing schools.

During <em>recess</em>, a younger student said Daniel had pushed him and taken his notebook. The <em>principal</em> called Daniel to the office, and a <em>counselor</em> joined the meeting to understand what had happened. Daniel admitted he had acted like a <em>bully</em> because some older students had been teasing him.

At first, the <em>principal</em> considered whether to <em>suspend</em> him for several days. Instead, Daniel received <em>detention</em> for a week, wrote an apology, and agreed to meet the <em>counselor</em> every Friday. The <em>principal</em> warned him that if he was <em>suspended</em> again for hurting someone, the school might <em>expel</em> him.

By the end of the month, Daniel was calmer and more respectful. His <em>teacher</em> said he still had consequences, but he was learning how to solve problems without breaking school rules.
