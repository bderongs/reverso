## Text

Airport <em>security</em> can feel stressful, but a little preparation makes the process faster. Before you join the line, keep your <em>passport</em> and <em>boarding pass</em> ready, and check that your <em>carry-on</em> meets <em>airline</em> size rules.

In the United States, <em>TSA</em> officers may ask passengers to remove shoes, jackets, belts, and electronics. Place your laptop, <em>tablet</em>, liquids bag, and other items in a separate <em>tray</em> so the machine can <em>scan</em> them clearly.

Some items are <em>prohibited</em> in <em>hand luggage</em>, including large bottles of liquids, sharp tools, and certain sports equipment. If you are unsure, check the airport or <em>airline</em> website before <em>packing</em>, because rules can vary by country.

Keep <em>valuables</em> such as cash, jewelry, and cameras inside your bag whenever possible, and watch your belongings as they move along the belt. If an officer needs to inspect something, stay calm and answer questions politely.

Occasionally, passengers are asked to step aside for extra screening, which may include another bag check or a pat down. This is usually routine and is meant to keep all travelers safe.
