## Text

A successful launch rarely depends on optimism alone. Before announcing a release date, project managers assess each <em>risk</em>, from supplier <em>delays</em> to software defects, and decide which <em>issues</em> need a <em>preemptive</em> response.

Teams often build a <em>contingency</em> <em>budget</em> and a <em>plan B</em> for critical <em>milestones</em>. In a <em>best-case</em> scenario, the product ships early and marketing gains extra time; in a <em>worst-case</em> situation, a major bug forces the company to delay customer access.

Clear reporting helps leaders spot a <em>red flag</em> before it becomes a crisis. For example, if testing data shows repeated payment failures, engineers can <em>troubleshoot</em> the system, apply a <em>workaround</em>, and continue investigating the root cause.

Every <em>decision</em> has an <em>upside</em> and a <em>downside</em>. The <em>upside</em> of delaying is better quality, while the <em>downside</em> may be lost publicity, so managers must <em>resolve</em> conflicts quickly and keep stakeholders informed.
