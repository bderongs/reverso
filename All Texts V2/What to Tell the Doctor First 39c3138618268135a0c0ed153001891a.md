## Text

When you visit a <em>clinic</em>, the first details you give can help the doctor understand your <em>condition</em> quickly. Start with your main <em>symptom</em>, when it began, and whether it is getting better, worse, or staying the same.

Be specific about discomfort: say where you feel an <em>ache</em>, how strong it is, and what makes it more <em>painful</em>. Mention common signs such as a <em>fever</em>, a <em>headache</em>, a <em>runny nose</em>, or a <em>sore throat</em>, even if they seem <em>minor</em>.

Also explain any digestive or balance problems, including an <em>upset stomach</em>, <em>nausea</em>, or <em>dizziness</em>. If you <em>cough</em> during the day or at night, describe whether it is dry or produces mucus.

Finally, tell the doctor about <em>fatigue</em>, sleep changes, recent travel, and any <em>medicine</em> you have already taken. Clear, honest information can lead to a faster <em>diagnosis</em> and more suitable <em>treatment</em>.
