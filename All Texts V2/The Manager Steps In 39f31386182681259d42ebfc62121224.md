## Text

By ten o’clock, the product meeting had reached a <em>deadlock</em>. Maya from sales wanted a faster launch, while Daniel from engineering warned that rushing could <em>escalate</em> customer complaints.

Their voices grew sharper until Priya, the manager, decided to <em>intervene</em>. Known as <em>fair-minded</em>, she asked both teams to <em>restate</em> their main <em>objective</em> and avoid blaming each other.

To <em>defuse</em> the tension, Priya helped them <em>reframe</em> the problem as a shared <em>risk</em> rather than a personal battle. She offered to <em>mediate</em> a short discussion focused only on what could be changed before Friday.

Daniel made a <em>concession</em> by <em>agreeing</em> to simplify one feature, and Maya accepted the <em>trade-off</em> of delaying the marketing email by two days. By the end, Priya had helped <em>resolve</em> the conflict and <em>bridge the divide</em> between the teams.
