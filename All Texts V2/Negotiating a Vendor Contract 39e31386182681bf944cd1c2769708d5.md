## Text

Last month, our project team needed a software vendor before the launch phase. During the first call, I asked for a <em>ballpark figure</em>, but their <em>estimate</em> was far above our <em>budget</em>.

In the negotiation meeting, our sponsor told me to <em>stand firm</em> on delivery dates and hold my ground on support costs. The vendor offered extra training to <em>sweeten the deal</em>, but we had to <em>draw the line</em> at a two-year <em>commitment</em>.

After an hour, both sides agreed to <em>meet halfway</em> on the service fee and <em>split the difference</em> on customization costs. We <em>made concessions</em> on payment terms, and they reduced the setup charge, creating a <em>win-win</em> arrangement.

By Friday, we were ready to <em>cut a deal</em> that protected our <em>timeline</em> and gave the vendor a strong reference client. Once legal approved the final wording, we were able to <em>seal the deal</em> and <em>close the deal</em> before the steering committee meeting.
