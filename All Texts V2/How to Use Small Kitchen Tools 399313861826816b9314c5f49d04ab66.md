## Text

Small kitchen tools can make everyday cooking quicker and neater when you know their purpose. A <em>grater</em> is ideal for cheese, carrots, or citrus zest, while a <em>slicer</em> helps you <em>cut</em> vegetables evenly for salads or stir-fries.

For <em>soft</em> foods, a <em>masher</em> turns boiled potatoes, beans, or bananas into a smooth mixture. When <em>draining</em> pasta or <em>rinsing</em> berries, use a <em>strainer</em>; for finer <em>ingredients</em> like flour or cocoa powder, a <em>sieve</em> gives better results.

Some tools are designed for opening and serving. A <em>can opener</em> is essential for <em>tins</em> of tomatoes or beans, a <em>bottle opener</em> works for capped drinks, and a <em>corkscrew</em> removes <em>corks</em> from wine bottles safely.

Accuracy also matters in the kitchen. A <em>measuring cup</em> helps you follow <em>recipes</em>, and a <em>jug</em> is useful for <em>mixing</em> batter, <em>pouring</em> stock, or serving water.

Finally, choose the right tool for handling food. A <em>spatula</em> flips pancakes or scrapes <em>bowls</em>, while <em>tongs</em> let you turn meat, serve salad, or lift hot vegetables without burning your fingers.
