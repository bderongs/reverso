## Text

A strong presentation is not just about showing numbers; it is about helping people <em>make sense of</em> them. In a <em>data-driven</em> meeting, the speaker’s job is to turn complex information into a clear <em>insight</em> that the audience can use.

Start with a simple <em>breakdown</em> of the key facts, then give a <em>ballpark figure</em> before moving into details. This helps put the results in perspective and allows listeners to <em>connect the dots</em> without feeling lost.

Good visuals can <em>shed light on</em> trends that are hard to explain in words. They may also <em>bring to light</em> unexpected patterns, such as a sudden drop in customer interest or a gap between departments.

Still, <em>the crux of the problem</em> is often not the data itself, but the story behind it. The <em>crux of the matter</em> is choosing what to <em>highlight</em> and what to leave out.

The <em>bottom line</em> is clear: effective presenters guide their audience from confusion to understanding. When data is organized well, decisions become faster and more confident.
