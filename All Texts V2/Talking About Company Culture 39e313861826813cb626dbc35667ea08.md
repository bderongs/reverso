## Text

During a job <em>interview</em>, <em>candidates</em> often focus on <em>salary</em> and duties, but employers also listen for cultural fit. A strong answer shows that you understand the company’s <em>mission</em>, respect its core <em>value</em>, and can connect your <em>skills</em> to its long-term <em>vision</em>.

When discussing culture, look beyond daily tasks and mention the <em>big picture</em>. For example, you might say that your <em>objective</em> is to help teams <em>deliver</em> practical results while also supporting the <em>bottom line</em> through smart, <em>efficient</em> decisions.

Interviewers may ask what kind of workplace helps you <em>thrive</em>. You can explain that you do your best in an <em>innovative</em> and <em>creative</em> environment where colleagues share ideas, give <em>insightful</em> feedback, and stay open to learning.

It is also useful to describe how you <em>envision</em> your role within the team. In a <em>competitive</em> market, companies appreciate <em>candidates</em> who can adapt, <em>collaborate</em>, and contribute to a positive culture from the first day.
