Subject: A quick note about <em>getting along</em> at work

Hi Maya,

Since you asked about becoming <em>friends</em> with colleagues, I’d say the key is <em>respect</em>. Be warm, but don’t feel you have to be a <em>people pleaser</em> or a <em>pushover</em> just to <em>fit in</em>.

It’s fine to be <em>assertive</em> when someone interrupts you or gives you extra tasks. You can say no politely; you don’t need to <em>put up with</em> behavior that feels unfair or <em>dismissive</em>.

At the same time, try not to <em>criticize</em> people too quickly. Some coworkers seem <em>standoffish</em> at first, but they may simply be shy or focused, not rude.

If someone is <em>blunt</em>, <em>listen</em> for the message, not just the tone. Still, if their comments become personal, <em>keep your distance</em> and speak to your manager if needed.

<em>Friendship</em> at work often depends on <em>compromise</em>: sharing space, adjusting plans, and knowing when to talk or stay <em>quiet</em>.

Best,
Daniel
