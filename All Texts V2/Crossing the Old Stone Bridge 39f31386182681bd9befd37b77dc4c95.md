## Text

Last spring, I arrived in Mostar just before sunset, when the narrow streets were still busy with travelers and café owners setting out chairs. The old <em>bridge</em> rose above the <em>river</em> with a graceful <em>arch</em>, and its pale stones seemed to glow against the warm evening light.

A local guide explained that the area is both a <em>historic site</em> and a protected <em>heritage site</em>, rebuilt with great care after the war. From the riverbank, the <em>towering</em> houses on the hillside created a <em>dramatic</em> <em>backdrop</em>, while the bridge’s dark <em>silhouette</em> slowly appeared in the fading sky.

I climbed to a small <em>terrace</em> for the best <em>view</em>, and the scene was <em>stunning</em>: clear water below, <em>charming</em> rooftops nearby, and <em>mountains</em> in the distance. Among all the places I visited that week, this one truly <em>stood out</em> because it felt alive, not just <em>beautiful</em>.
