## Text

When shopping for clothes, small details often reveal whether a <em>garment</em> is worth buying. Check the <em>seam</em> lines first: they should lie flat, feel <em>smooth</em>, and show no <em>loose</em> threads.

A <em>jacket</em> or <em>dress</em> with a <em>zipper</em> should open easily, while a trouser <em>zip</em> must stay firmly closed. Test each <em>button</em> as well, because weak stitching can make a new <em>item</em> look <em>worn</em> after only a few uses.

Look closely at the <em>collar</em>, <em>sleeve</em>, and <em>cuff</em> to judge both <em>style</em> and comfort. A neat <em>collar</em> can make a <em>shirt</em> look smarter, and a well-shaped <em>cuff</em> can improve how the <em>sleeve</em> sits on your wrist.

Accessories and decoration matter too. A strong <em>buckle</em> on a <em>belt</em>, <em>delicate</em> <em>lace</em> on a <em>blouse</em>, or colorful <em>trim</em> on a <em>skirt</em> can change the whole impression of an <em>outfit</em>.

Finally, never skip the <em>fitting</em> room. During a <em>fitting</em>, move around, sit down, and raise your arms to make sure the clothes match your body and your daily life.
