## Text

A neighborhood cafe on Maple Street is struggling to <em>make ends meet</em> as rent, coffee beans, and electricity bills rise. The owner says a new espresso machine would <em>cost an arm and a leg</em>, but the old one breaks down almost every week.

For years, weekend brunch was the cafe’s <em>cash cow</em>, helping the business <em>break even</em> during slow months. Now, fewer customers are coming in, and the cafe has been <em>in the red</em> since January instead of safely <em>in the black</em>.

A delivery app seemed like a solution, but it became a <em>double-edged sword</em> because high fees reduced profits. The owner admits she did not <em>read the fine print</em> before signing the contract.

Local permits and other <em>red tape</em> have also delayed plans to sell pastries outside. If sales do not improve soon, she may have to <em>pull the plug</em> on evening hours to avoid <em>going belly up</em>.

Still, regular customers are organizing a weekly coffee club to support the cafe. They believe small changes could save money <em>in the long run</em>.
