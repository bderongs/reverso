## Text

On a quiet side street, a new independent boutique is attracting shoppers who prefer personal service to crowded department stores. In the front window, a <em>mannequin</em> <em>wears</em> a <em>vintage</em> <em>silk</em> <em>blouse</em>, <em>tailored</em> trousers, and a <em>chic</em> <em>wool</em> <em>coat</em>.

Inside, every <em>hanger</em> is arranged by color, from <em>soft</em> cream to deep navy. A wooden <em>clothes hanger</em> holds a <em>high-end</em> <em>blazer</em> that the owner says was made by a local designer.

Near the <em>fitting</em> room, a <em>velvet</em> <em>curtain</em> gives customers privacy, while a brass <em>hook</em> on the wall keeps each <em>item</em> off the floor. Staff members offer honest advice about <em>size</em>, <em>fabric</em>, and how to style a piece for work or a weekend dinner.

Accessories are displayed on a <em>narrow</em> table: a <em>leather</em> <em>purse</em>, a structured <em>handbag</em>, and a small <em>satin</em> <em>clutch</em> for evening events. The shop’s careful mix of modern basics and unique finds makes clothes shopping feel relaxed, thoughtful, and a little special.
