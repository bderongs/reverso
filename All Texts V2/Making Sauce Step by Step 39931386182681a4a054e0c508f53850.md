## Text

A good homemade sauce begins with patience and a heavy <em>pot</em>. Place it over medium heat, add a little butter, and let it <em>melt</em> gently before adding chopped onions or garlic.

<em>Stir</em> the mixture often so the vegetables soften without turning <em>burnt</em>. When the aroma becomes <em>savory</em>, add tomatoes, stock, or cream, depending on the sauce you want.

Let the sauce <em>simmer</em> slowly, because quick boiling can make the flavor sharp or <em>sour</em>. If foam rises to the surface, <em>skim</em> it off with a <em>spoon</em> to keep the texture smooth and clean.

For a <em>creamy</em> finish, <em>blend</em> the sauce until it looks even and glossy. Taste it at the end, then adjust the salt, herbs, or pepper to create a <em>rich</em> flavor that works well with pasta, rice, or roasted vegetables.
