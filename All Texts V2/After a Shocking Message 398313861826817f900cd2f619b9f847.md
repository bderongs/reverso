## Text

On Monday morning, I was <em>surprised</em> to see a message from my old neighbor, Mrs. Harris, whom I hadn’t heard from in years. When I opened it, I was <em>shocked</em> to read that she had found a box of my childhood letters in her attic.

At first, I felt <em>puzzled</em> because I couldn’t remember writing to anyone at her address. Then I became <em>confused</em> and even <em>baffled</em> when she sent a photo of envelopes covered in my messy ten-year-old handwriting.

My mother looked at the picture and seemed <em>bewildered</em> for a moment, then she laughed. She explained that I had once joined a school project where we wrote “future letters” and gave them to adults to keep.

I was <em>startled</em> by how emotional I felt reading them, and some lines left me <em>perplexed</em> and <em>dumbfounded</em>. By the end, I was <em>rattled</em> but also <em>amazed</em>; those simple letters made me feel <em>shaken</em>, <em>grateful</em>, and strangely close to my younger self.
