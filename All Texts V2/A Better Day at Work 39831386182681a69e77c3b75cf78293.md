## Text

On Monday morning, Lena walked into the office feeling <em>nervous</em> about presenting her project. She had worked late all week, and although she was tired, she felt <em>motivated</em> to show what her team had achieved.

During the meeting, her manager listened carefully and smiled. When the presentation ended, everyone clapped, and Lena felt <em>relieved</em>, <em>proud</em>, and more <em>confident</em> than she had expected.

Later, a colleague thanked her for explaining a difficult problem so clearly. Lena was <em>grateful</em> for the kind words and <em>thankful</em> that her effort was noticed; the feeling of being appreciated made the whole week seem worthwhile.

By the end of the day, she felt <em>satisfied</em> with her progress and <em>fulfilled</em> by her contribution. As she left the building, she was <em>joyful</em>, <em>delighted</em> by the support around her, and <em>thrilled</em> to begin the next project.
